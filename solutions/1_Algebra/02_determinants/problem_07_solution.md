
## Exercise 7 — Solution

Given a 3×3 matrix with:

$$
\det(A)=-4
$$

We need to calculate four determinants using their properties.

**Step 1: Calculate det(2A)**

For an n×n matrix:

$$
\det(cA)=c^n\det(A)
$$

Since A is a 3×3 matrix:

$$
\det(2A)=2^3\cdot(-4)
$$

$$
=8\cdot(-4)
$$

$$
\boxed{\det(2A)=-32}
$$

**Step 2: Calculate det(A^T)**

The determinant of a transpose is equal to the determinant of the original matrix.

$$
\det(A^T)=\det(A)
$$

$$
\boxed{\det(A^T)=-4}
$$

**Step 3: Calculate det(A²)**

We use the product property:

$$
\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\det(A^2)=(\det(A))^2
$$

$$
=(-4)^2
$$

$$
\boxed{\det(A^2)=16}
$$

**Step 4: Calculate det(-A)**

Since A is a 3×3 matrix:

$$
\det(-A)=(-1)^3\det(A)
$$

$$
=(-1)(-4)
$$

$$
\boxed{\det(-A)=4}
$$

**Conclusion:**

The final answers are:

$$
\det(2A)=-32
$$

$$
\det(A^T)=-4
$$

$$
\det(A^2)=16
$$

$$
\det(-A)=4
$$
